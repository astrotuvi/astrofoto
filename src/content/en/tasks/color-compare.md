# Star colours

Find one bluish and one reddish (orange) star in the colour image. Do not choose a star so bright that all channels are fully bright at its centre. Move the mouse to the centre of each star and read the values of the stacked **{blue}** and **{green}** images from the pixel readout.

Which star is relatively brighter in the {blue} filter? What does this tell about the star's temperature?

If you have images with two filter sets (e.g. BVR and gri), make a colour image of both. Why do they differ?

<!-- hint -->
On a colour image the pixel readout shows the values of all channels at once. Move the mouse around the centre of the star until you find the largest value.

<!-- hint -->
For each star, compute the ratio: the value in the {blue} filter divided by the value in the {green} filter. Compare the ratios of the two stars. Which one is larger?

<!-- hint -->
A hot star gives off more short-wavelength (blue) light, a cooler star more long-wavelength (red) light. So the bluish star is relatively brighter in the blue filter, and its ratio is larger. Astronomers measure this with the **colour index** B − V: the difference of the star's brightness in magnitudes in the B and V filters. The smaller B − V, the hotter the star. The BVR and gri filters let through slightly different wavelengths (see the table of wavelengths), so the colours of the two images differ a little.
